Lean Statement

(a * b)⁻¹ = b⁻¹ * a⁻¹
Read this as: to undo the product “first $a$, then $b$”, undo $b$ first and $a$ second. Inverses reverse the order of multiplication.

To show that $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ is the inverse of $ab$, multiply them and simplify:

\[ \begin{aligned} (ab)(b^{-1}a^{-1}) &=a(bb^{-1})a^{-1}\\ &=a1a^{-1}\\ &=1. \end{aligned} \]

A symmetric calculation gives $(b^{-1}a^{-1})(ab)=1$. By uniqueness of inverses, $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ must equal $(ab)^{-1}$. Notice that no commutativity is used: only associativity and the inverse laws.

Why This Proof Matters

This proof introduces basic group reasoning: associativity, inverses, and the difference between a manual proof and group automation.

Schematic Outline

  1. Work in an arbitrary group.
  2. Regard $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ as the candidate inverse of $ab$.
  3. Prove directly that $(ab)(b^{-1}a^{-1})=1$ by reassociating the factors and cancelling adjacent inverse pairs.
  4. Insert this right-inverse calculation next to the known inverse $(ab)^{-1}$, then cancel $(ab)^{-1}(ab)$.

Lean Proof

import Mathlib

theorem inverse_of_product {G : Type u} [Group G] (a b : G) :
    (a * b)⁻¹ = b⁻¹ * a⁻¹ := by
  -- First step: establish the helper fact that abb⁻¹a⁻¹ = 1, i.e. b⁻¹ a⁻¹ is
  -- the right inverse of ab
  have hright : (a * b) * (b⁻¹ * a⁻¹) = 1 := by
    calc
      (a * b) * (b⁻¹ * a⁻¹) = a * (b * b⁻¹) * a⁻¹ := by
        simp only [mul_assoc]
      _ = a * 1 * a⁻¹ := by
        rw [mul_inv_cancel]
      _ = a * a⁻¹ := by
        rw [mul_one]
      _ = 1 := by
        rw [mul_inv_cancel]
  -- Second step: show that (ab)⁻¹ is b⁻¹ a⁻¹.
  calc
    (a * b)⁻¹ = (a * b)⁻¹ * 1 := by
      rw [mul_one]
    _ = (a * b)⁻¹ * ((a * b) * (b⁻¹ * a⁻¹)) := by
      rw [hright]
    _ = ((a * b)⁻¹ * (a * b)) * (b⁻¹ * a⁻¹) := by
      rw [← mul_assoc]
    _ = 1 * (b⁻¹ * a⁻¹) := by
      rw [inv_mul_cancel]
    _ = b⁻¹ * a⁻¹ := by
      rw [one_mul]

Proof Breakdown

[Group G]
Gives Lean multiplication, identity, inverses, associativity, and the inverse laws for G.
(a * b)⁻¹
The inverse of the product, not the product of the inverses in the original order.
b⁻¹ * a⁻¹
The candidate inverse $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ obtained by reversing the order of the factors.
hright
Proves directly that $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ is a right inverse of $ab$.
mul_assoc
Reassociates factors so that $bb^{-1}$ becomes an adjacent pair; it never changes their order.
mul_inv_cancel and inv_mul_cancel
Apply the two inverse laws only after the relevant factors have been made adjacent.

Automation and the Native Proof

The main Lean proof follows the displayed calculation explicitly. The native version below goes one step further: it defines a tiny group structure so that the only available facts are exactly the axioms used by the proof. In both versions, the key move is to name $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ as the candidate and prove that it is a right inverse of $ab$.

Native Lean Version

Lean itself does not ship with mathlib's group hierarchy, but the identity can be proved in native Lean after defining the tiny fragment of group structure needed for the argument.

structure NativeGroup where
  carrier : Type
  mul : carrier → carrier → carrier
  one : carrier
  inv : carrier → carrier
  mul_assoc : ∀ a b c, mul (mul a b) c = mul a (mul b c)
  one_mul : ∀ a, mul one a = a
  mul_one : ∀ a, mul a one = a
  inv_mul_cancel : ∀ a, mul (inv a) a = one
  mul_inv_cancel : ∀ a, mul a (inv a) = one

namespace NativeGroup

variable (G : NativeGroup)

theorem native_inverse_of_product (a b : G.carrier) :
    G.inv (G.mul a b) = G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a) := by
  let candidate := G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a)

  have hright : G.mul (G.mul a b) candidate = G.one := by
    change G.mul (G.mul a b) (G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a)) = G.one
    calc
      G.mul (G.mul a b) (G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a))
          = G.mul a (G.mul b (G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a))) := by
              rw [G.mul_assoc a b (G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a))]
      _ = G.mul a (G.mul (G.mul b (G.inv b)) (G.inv a)) := by
              rw [← G.mul_assoc b (G.inv b) (G.inv a)]
      _ = G.mul a (G.mul G.one (G.inv a)) := by
              rw [G.mul_inv_cancel]
      _ = G.mul a (G.inv a) := by
              rw [G.one_mul]
      _ = G.one := by
              rw [G.mul_inv_cancel]

  calc
    G.inv (G.mul a b)
        = G.mul (G.inv (G.mul a b)) G.one := by
            rw [G.mul_one]
    _ = G.mul (G.inv (G.mul a b)) (G.mul (G.mul a b) candidate) := by
            rw [hright]
    _ = G.mul (G.mul (G.inv (G.mul a b)) (G.mul a b)) candidate := by
            rw [← G.mul_assoc (G.inv (G.mul a b)) (G.mul a b) candidate]
    _ = G.mul G.one candidate := by
            rw [G.inv_mul_cancel]
    _ = candidate := by
            rw [G.one_mul]
    _ = G.mul (G.inv b) (G.inv a) := rfl

end NativeGroup

Caveats

For the Mathlib proof:

This is horrendously verbose and could be compactified into a line or two. This is also a fairly trivial lemma available in Mathlib. In fact, such a proof is exactly the type that would be given by AI a few years ago when it was in its infancy. I chose to do it in such a round-about way as a means of showing off the `calc` tactic which I don't think we've seen so far. It's nice to be able to convert a pen-and-paper proof into something in Lean; albeit, I'd certainly NOT do this in production though except in special circumstances.

The explicit proof is useful when the cancellation order is the lesson. In ordinary Lean mathematics, the same identity can be shortened to by group; that is an appropriate caveat when this group calculation is only a supporting step.
