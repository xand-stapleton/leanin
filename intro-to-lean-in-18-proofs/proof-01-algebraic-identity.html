Lean Statement

(x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2*x*y + y^2
Read this as: for any integers $x$ and $y$, squaring their sum gives the two squares together with the two cross terms.

By definition, $(x+y)^2=(x+y)(x+y)$. Distributing the first factor gives $x(x+y)+y(x+y)$, and distributing once more gives $x^2+xy+yx+y^2$. Because integer multiplication is commutative, the two middle terms combine to $2xy$.

Why This Proof Matters

This proof shows how Lean handles routine algebra (so long as you specify the field etc).

Schematic Outline

  1. Declare $x$ and $y$ to be integers, so all the overloaded arithmetic notation has a definite meaning.
  2. State the identity as an equality of ring expressions.
  3. Ask Lean's ring normaliser to expand, reorder, and collect terms on both sides.
  4. Close the goal when the two normal forms agree.

Lean Proof

import Mathlib

/--
The expansion of `(x + y)^2`.

The Lean-specific step is choosing a type where `ring` can normalise polynomial
expressions.
-/
theorem algebraic_identity (x y : ℤ) :
    (x + y) ^ 2 = x ^ 2 + 2 * x * y + y ^ 2 := by
  -- `ring` expands, reassociates, reorders, and collects polynomial terms.
  ring

Proof Breakdown

ℤ
Uses the integers as the ambient type, giving Lean a concrete commutative ring to work in.
^
Writes powers. Here (x + y) ^ 2 represents the square $(x+y)^2$.
:= by
Starts tactic mode, where the proof is written as a sequence of commands transforming goals.
ring
Solves equalities between polynomial expressions in semirings and rings by normalising both sides. It is a proof-producing tactic: Lean's kernel still checks the proof term it constructs.

Caveat

This version proves the identity over ℤ. The same algebraic identity can be stated more generally over any suitable semiring or ring, but the concrete version keeps the first example focused on the ring tactic.
