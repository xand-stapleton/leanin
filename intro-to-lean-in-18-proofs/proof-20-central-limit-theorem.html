Lean Statement

TendstoInDistribution
  (fun (n : ℕ) ω ↦ (√n)⁻¹ * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω)
  atTop Y (fun _ ↦ P) P'
Read this as: let $X_0,X_1,\ldots$ be independent, identically distributed real random variables. If $X_0$ has mean $0$ and second moment $1$ (hence variance $1$), then the normalised sums $\frac{X_0+\cdots+X_{n-1}}{\sqrt n}$ converge in distribution to a standard Gaussian random variable $Y$.

For a fixed real $t$, write $\varphi(t)=\mathbb E[e^{itX_0}]$. Independence and identical distribution give

\[ \varphi_{S_n/\sqrt n}(t)=\varphi(t/\sqrt n)^n. \]

The mean and second-moment assumptions give the local expansion

\[ \varphi(u)=1-\frac{u^2}{2}+o(u^2) \qquad (u\to0). \]

Putting $u=t/\sqrt n$ turns this into $1-\frac{t^2}{2n}+o(1/n)$. Therefore the $n$th power tends to $e^{-t^2/2}$, the characteristic function of $N(0,1)$. Lévy's continuity theorem then converts that pointwise statement into convergence in distribution.

Why This Proof Matters

The central limit theorem explains why Gaussian distributions recur in data, physics, and statistics. Closer to my heart is the Neural Network Field Theory correspondence, which the CLT (or a generalisation of the CLT) underpins. More importantly for Lean, it illustrates a dependable formalisation pattern: turn a statement about laws into an equality or limit of characteristic functions; separate the probability calculation from the analytic estimate; then use a continuity theorem to return to distributions.

The write-up follows the characteristic-function proof in Patrick Billingsley, Probability and Measure, 3rd ed., Wiley, 1995, Section 27, “The Central Limit Theorem.” Billingsley explicitly obtains the characteristic function $\varphi^n(t/\sqrt n)$ and finishes with the continuity theorem; see the standard non-Lean proof. The Lean proof below uses the same mathematical route but has been factored into page-specific lemmas and comments.

Schematic Outline

  1. Define the normalised finite sum $S_n/\sqrt n$.
  2. Use independence to factor its characteristic function, then use identical distribution to make the product an $n$th power.
  3. Compose the second-order Taylor remainder for $\varphi$ with $n\mapsto t/\sqrt n$.
  4. Rewrite the resulting scale as a constant multiple of $1/n$ and apply the complex exponential-limit lemma.
  5. Recognise the limit as the characteristic function of `gaussianReal 0 1`.
  6. Apply Lévy's continuity theorem to obtain convergence in distribution.

Lean Proof

import Mathlib.Probability.Distributions.Gaussian.Real
import Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Function.ConvergenceInDistribution

import Mathlib.Probability.Independence.CharacteristicFunction

open MeasureTheory ProbabilityTheory Complex Filter
open scoped Real Topology

noncomputable section

namespace CLTPage

variable {Ω Ω' : Type*}
variable {mΩ : MeasurableSpace Ω} {mΩ' : MeasurableSpace Ω'}
variable {P : Measure Ω} {P' : Measure Ω'}
variable {X : ℕ → Ω → ℝ} {Y : Ω' → ℝ}

/-- Naming the normalised sum keeps the final theorem and the intermediate
    characteristic-function calculation readable. -/
def normalisedSum (X : ℕ → Ω → ℝ) (n : ℕ) (ω : Ω) : ℝ :=
  (√n)⁻¹ * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω

/--
Independence supplies the product formula. Identical distribution says that
every factor has the law of `X 0`, so the product is a power. This is the
probability-specific part of the proof; no limiting argument happens here.
-/
lemma charFun_normalisedSum
    (h_indep : iIndepFun X P)
    (h_law : ∀ k : ℕ, IdentDistrib (X k) (X 0) P P)
    (n : ℕ) (t : ℝ) :
    charFun (P.map (normalisedSum X n)) t =
      (charFun (P.map (X 0)) ((√n)⁻¹ * t)) ^ n := by
  -- `IdentDistrib` also gives the a.e.-measurability needed by the product lemma.
  have h_measurable : ∀ k : ℕ, AEMeasurable (X k) P :=
    fun k => (h_law k).aemeasurable_fst
  change charFun (P.map (fun ω ↦ (√n)⁻¹ * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω)) t = _
  have h_sum_measurable :
      AEMeasurable (fun ω ↦ ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω) P :=
    Finset.aemeasurable_fun_sum _ fun k _ ↦ h_measurable k
  calc
    -- Scaling a variable replaces `t` by `(√n)⁻¹ * t` in its characteristic function.
    charFun (P.map (fun ω ↦ (√n)⁻¹ * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω)) t =
        charFun (P.map (fun ω ↦ ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω)) ((√n)⁻¹ * t) :=
      charFun_map_mul_comp h_sum_measurable _ _
    -- Independence factors the characteristic function of the finite sum.
    _ = ∏ k ∈ Finset.range n, charFun (P.map (X k)) ((√n)⁻¹ * t) := by
      simpa only [Finset.prod_apply] using congrFun
        ((h_indep.restrict (Finset.range n)).charFun_map_fun_finsetSum_eq_prod
          (fun k _ ↦ h_measurable k)) ((√n)⁻¹ * t)
    -- Equal laws turn the product of identical factors into an `n`th power.
    _ = (charFun (P.map (X 0)) ((√n)⁻¹ * t)) ^ n := by
      simp [fun k ↦ (h_law k).map_eq]

variable [IsProbabilityMeasure P]

/--
The Taylor remainder is first proved near zero and then composed with the
CLT scale `n ↦ t / √n`. Keeping this as a separate lemma makes explicit
which part of the proof uses the moment assumptions.
-/
lemma scaled_taylor_remainder
    {Z : Ω → ℝ} (hZ : AEMeasurable Z P)
    (h_mean : P[Z] = 0) (h_secondMoment : P[Z ^ 2] = 1)
    (t : ℝ) :
    (fun n : ℕ ↦ charFun (P.map Z) ((√n)⁻¹ * t) -
      (1 + (-(((√n)⁻¹ * t) ^ 2 / 2) : ℂ))) =o[atTop]
      fun n ↦ ((√n)⁻¹ * t) ^ 2 := by
  have h_scale : Tendsto (fun n : ℕ ↦ (√n)⁻¹ * t) atTop (𝓝 0) := by
    -- Because `√n → ∞`, its reciprocal tends to zero.
    rw [← zero_mul t]
    exact (tendsto_inv_atTop_zero.comp
      (Real.tendsto_sqrt_atTop.comp tendsto_natCast_atTop_atTop)).mul_const t
  -- Compose Mathlib's local Taylor estimate with the scale just established.
  convert (taylor_charFun_two hZ h_mean h_secondMoment).comp_tendsto h_scale using 2
  · simp
    ring
  · rfl

/--
The analytic step in the textbook proof: after rescaling, the Taylor error is
`o(1/n)`, so the standard complex limit `(1 + z / n + o(1/n))^n → exp z`
applies with `z = -t^2/2`.
-/
lemma charFun_power_tendsto_gaussian
    {Z : Ω → ℝ} (hZ : AEMeasurable Z P)
    (h_mean : P[Z] = 0) (h_secondMoment : P[Z ^ 2] = 1)
    (t : ℝ) :
    Tendsto (fun n : ℕ ↦ (charFun (P.map Z) ((√n)⁻¹ * t)) ^ n)
      atTop (𝓝 (exp (-t ^ 2 / 2))) := by
  apply tendsto_pow_exp_of_isLittleO_sub_add_div
  have h_remainder := scaled_taylor_remainder hZ h_mean h_secondMoment t
  have h_norm_scale :
      (fun n : ℕ ↦ ‖(1 / n : ℂ)‖) = (fun n : ℕ ↦ ‖(1 / n : ℝ)‖) := by
    simp
  -- The next rewrites express little-o in norms. The algebra below identifies
  -- `(t / √n)^2` with the fixed multiple `t^2 * (1 / n)`.
  rw [← Asymptotics.isLittleO_norm_right, h_norm_scale,
    Asymptotics.isLittleO_norm_right]
  refine Asymptotics.IsLittleO.of_const_mul_right (c := t ^ 2) ?_
  convert h_remainder using 4 with n <;> norm_cast <;> simp [field]

variable [IsProbabilityMeasure P']

/-- The CLT is now just the assembly of the preceding probability and analysis lemmas. -/
theorem central_limit_theorem_page
    (hY : HasLaw Y (gaussianReal 0 1) P')
    (h_mean : P[X 0] = 0) (h_secondMoment : P[X 0 ^ 2] = 1)
    (h_indep : iIndepFun X P)
    (h_law : ∀ k : ℕ, IdentDistrib (X k) (X 0) P P) :
    TendstoInDistribution (normalisedSum X) atTop Y (fun _ ↦ P) P' where
  -- A normalised finite sum is measurable because each summand is measurable.
  forall_aemeasurable n := by
    change AEMeasurable (fun ω ↦ (√n)⁻¹ * ∑ k ∈ Finset.range n, X k ω) P
    exact AEMeasurable.const_mul
      (Finset.aemeasurable_fun_sum _ fun k _ ↦ (h_law k).aemeasurable_fst) _
  tendsto := by
    -- Lévy reduces convergence of laws to pointwise convergence of charFuns.
    refine ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun.mpr fun t ↦ ?_
    rw! [hY.map_eq]
    -- The two sides are exactly the previous power limit and the Gaussian charFun.
    simpa [charFun_normalisedSum h_indep h_law, charFun_gaussianReal, neg_div] using
      charFun_power_tendsto_gaussian (h_law 0).aemeasurable_fst h_mean h_secondMoment t

end CLTPage

Proof Breakdown

1. Calculate the finite-sum characteristic function. `charFun_normalisedSum` has a three-line `calc` proof. The first line moves the scalar $1/\sqrt n$ from the random variable to the argument $t$. The second is the finite independence product formula. The third replaces all product factors using `IdentDistrib` and evaluates the product of a constant over `Finset.range n`.

2. Keep the asymptotic substitution visible. `scaled_taylor_remainder` is deliberately separate. `taylor_charFun_two` gives a statement at $u\to0$; `h_scale` proves that $t/\sqrt n\to0$; and `comp_tendsto` performs exactly the substitution $u=t/\sqrt n$ from the paper proof.

3. Use the elementary power limit. The only slightly technical Lean passage rewrites little-$o$ in norm form. It records the elementary identity $(t/\sqrt n)^2=t^2/n$, allowing `tendsto_pow_exp_of_isLittleO_sub_add_div` to recognise the pattern $(1+z/n+o(1/n))^n$ with $z=-t^2/2$.

4. Return to probability laws. `charFun_gaussianReal` identifies the limit with the standard Gaussian's characteristic function. `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun` is Lévy's continuity theorem in the form needed here, so its application proves convergence in distribution rather than merely convergence of transforms.

Caveat

This is not a proof of Lévy's continuity theorem or of the Taylor expansion from first principles. This uses those lower-level theorems, together with the finite independence product formula and the complex exponential limit. What it does not use is Mathlib's packaged central-limit-theorem file or theorem: the CLT-specific factorisation, rescaling, and final assembly are displayed here.
