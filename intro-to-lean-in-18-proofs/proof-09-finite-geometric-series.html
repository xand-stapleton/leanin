Lean Statement

(x - 1) * ∑ i < n, x^i = x^n - 1
Read this as: multiplying $1+x+\cdots+x^{n-1}$ by $x-1$ makes all the middle terms cancel, leaving $x^n-1$.

For example, when $n=4$,

\[ \begin{aligned} (x-1)(1+x+x^2+x^3) &=(x+x^2+x^3+x^4)-(1+x+x^2+x^3)\\ &=x^4-1. \end{aligned} \]

The proof below uses induction rather than expanding the whole sum at once. The successor step appends one term $x^n$ and then reuses the identity for the previous $n$ terms.

Why This Proof Matters

This proof reuses the induction pattern from the previous example, with powers adding one extra rewrite step in the successor case.

Schematic Outline

  1. Prove the multiplication form of the geometric series identity, avoiding division.
  2. Induct on the number of terms.
  3. Split the successor sum into the previous sum plus the new power.
  4. Use the induction hypothesis on the previous sum.
  5. Rewrite $x^{n+1}$ as $x^n x$ and normalise the remaining algebra.

Lean Proof

import Mathlib

/--
The finite geometric series identity, written without division:
`(x - 1) * (1 + x + ... + x^(n - 1)) = x^n - 1`.
-/
theorem finite_geometric_series (x : ℤ) (n : ℕ) :
    (x - 1) * (∑ i ∈ Finset.range n, x ^ i) = x ^ n - 1 := by
  induction n with
  | zero =>
      -- The empty sum is `0`, and `x^0 - 1` is also `0`.
      simp
  | succ n ih =>
      -- Rewrite the successor range sum as the previous range sum plus `x^n`.
      rw [Finset.sum_range_succ]

      calc
        (x - 1) * ((∑ i ∈ Finset.range n, x ^ i) + x ^ n)
            = (x - 1) * (∑ i ∈ Finset.range n, x ^ i) + (x - 1) * x ^ n := by
              ring
        _ = (x ^ n - 1) + (x - 1) * x ^ n := by
              rw [ih]
        _ = x ^ (n + 1) - 1 := by
              rw [pow_succ]
              ring

Uses the number of terms as the induction parameter.
Finset.sum_range_succ
Turns the sum over range (n + 1) into the sum over range n plus the new term at index $n$.
rw [ih]
Replaces the previous geometric sum using the induction hypothesis.
rw [pow_succ]
Rewrites $x^{n+1}$ as $x^n x$, putting the expression into a form that ring can normalise.

The first equality distributes $x-1$ over the enlarged sum. The induction hypothesis then collapses the entire old part to $x^n-1$. What remains is the one-line cancellation

\[(x^n-1)+(x-1)x^n=x^{n+1}-1.\]

pow_succ exposes the multiplication inside the new power, and ring checks that final polynomial calculation.

Caveat

This is the division-free form of the geometric series identity, stated over $\mathbb Z$. The more common formula $\frac{x^n-1}{x-1}$ needs extra assumptions about division and about $x\ne1$.
