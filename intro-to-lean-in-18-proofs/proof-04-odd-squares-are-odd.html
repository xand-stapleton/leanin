Lean Statement

Odd n → Odd (n^2)
Read this as: if a natural number $n$ has the form “twice a number plus one”, then its square also has that form.

Suppose $n=2k+1$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} n^2 &= (2k+1)^2 \\ &= 4k^2+4k+1 \\ &= 2(2k^2+2k)+1. \end{aligned} \]

The last line does a couple of things. Firstly, it says the square is odd; secondly it exhibits the witness $2k^2+2k$, which is precisely what Lean asks us to provide.

Why This Proof Matters

This proof shows how to unpack an existential hypothesis and then provide a new witness for an existential goal.

Schematic Outline

  1. Unpack the hypothesis that $n$ is odd into a witness $k$ and an equation for $n$.
  2. Notice that the goal is another existential statement, now asking for a witness for the oddness of $n^2$.
  3. Choose $2k^2+2k$ as that witness.
  4. Rewrite $n$ using the oddness equation.
  5. Finish by polynomial arithmetic.

Lean Proof

import Mathlib

/--
The square of an odd natural number is odd.

The proof turns the hypothesis `Odd n` into data, then supplies a witness for
`Odd (n ^ 2)`.
-/
theorem odd_square_is_odd (n : ℕ) :
    Odd n → Odd (n ^ 2) := by
  -- Unpack the existential hidden inside `Odd n`.
  rintro ⟨k, hk⟩

  -- If `n = 2*k + 1`, then `n^2 = 2*(2*k*k + 2*k) + 1`.
  use 2 * k * k + 2 * k

  -- Replace `n` by its odd form and let arithmetic finish.
  rw [hk]
  ring

Proof Breakdown

Odd n
Means there exists some witness $k$ with $n=2k+1$.
rintro ⟨k, hk⟩
Introduces the oddness hypothesis and immediately destructures it into a witness k and equation hk.
use ...
Supplies the witness required by an existential goal. After this line, Lean no longer asks whether some witness exists; it asks us to verify the equation for this particular choice.
rw [hk]
Rewrites the goal using the equation that describes n.
ring
Checks the remaining polynomial identity.

This proof is a useful model for statements of the form $(\exists k, P(k))\Rightarrow(\exists m,Q(m))$. The hypothesis is evidence we may unpack; the conclusion is evidence we must build. Here rintro performs the unpacking, and use begins the construction.

Caveat

The explicit proof above is useful for learning how existential witnesses work. In ordinary mathlib code, the general theorem Odd.pow can handle that witness internally:

/--
Library-style version: powers of odd elements are already known to be odd.
-/
theorem odd_square_is_odd_via_mathlib (n : ℕ) (hn : Odd n) :
    Odd (n ^ 2) := by
  exact hn.pow

hn.pow is dot notation for Odd.pow hn. It proves that $n^e$ is odd for any natural exponent $e$, and Lean infers $e=2$ from the goal. The existential witness still exists inside the proof of the library theorem; this version simply reuses that abstraction instead of constructing it again.
